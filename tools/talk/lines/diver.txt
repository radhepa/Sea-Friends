## diver.chat
Tidepearls grow from a speck of prismarine crystal tucked into a clam and sung to for a whole moon. Sing badly, you get a lumpy pearl. Sing well, you get a moonwhite.
There's three grades of Tidepearl: moonwhite, dawnblush and deepblue. Deepblue's the rarest. You only get one if the clam's had a sad moon.
I chart the reef as I dive. Every rock, every drop, every cave the fry aren't allowed in. My charts are prettier than the reef, honestly.
The best pearls come from near the shelf, where the water's cold. Cold makes them think. Thinking makes them shine.
Nautilus shells wash up on the reef now and then. A good one's worth a whole basin of pearls to a shellwright.
I can hold a pearl to the light and tell you which basin it came from. It's not a talent. It's just staring at pearls for too long.
The Weeping Pearl was the greatest Tidepearl ever grown. Black as the deep, and wet even out of water. It went down with Lowmere.
Divers never whistle at the drop. Never. I knew one who did. Came back with their hair gone white. They said it was the cold. Nobody believed them.
People think diving is all swimming down. It's mostly swimming down and then trying to remember what you came for.
The clams in my basins each have names. That one's Grumbles. That one's Lady Pout. They know when I'm fond of them, I'm sure of it.
I gather prismarine shards off the reef floor. They come up from the deep on the cold currents, like little letters from the Prismari.
If you ever find a clam the size of a cottage, back away. That's a grandmother clam, and she's not in the mood.
It's quiet at the bottom of a dive. Quiet like a held breath. I love it and I'm scared of it, and both are true.
A pearl takes a moon to grow and a moment to drop. I've chased more pearls into the sand than I've ever sold.
Pearls go dull if you leave them up in the dry too long. Same with merfolk, I'd say.

## diver.work
Pearl diving's simple: dive, find, sing, wait, wait some more, polish. The waiting's the hard part.
I've got three basins going this moon. One's a moonwhite, one's a maybe, and one's sulking.
When I'm not diving, I'm sorting shells. Big ones for the shellwrights, small ones for the fry, broken ones for the garden paths.
The secret to diving is never to look straight down at the drop. Look at your hands. Your hands are familiar. The drop is not.
I sell sea charts too. Every rock, every current, every spot a drowned's been seen. They're cheap, and they'll save your life.
You have to sing to the clams every morning. Not loudly. Clams hate loud. A hum's fine. A hum and a compliment.
Prismarine shards are worth gathering. The singers want them, the shellwrights want them, everyone wants a bit of the deep.
Bad day today. Dropped a dawnblush in the basin weeds and spent the whole afternoon apologising to the clam.
A pearl basin has to be kept spotless. One bit of rot and the whole moon's work goes cloudy.
If you find me in a bad mood, a clam's been sulking. If you find me singing, a pearl's come good.
You want a good pearl? Come back at the full moon. That's when they're ready, and that's when I'm generous.
I dive on a rope of knotted kelp: one knot for every body-length down. I've never gone past the twentieth knot. Nobody sensible has.
I'll trade pearls for anything from the dry. Last moon I got a spoon. A real land spoon. I don't know what it's for, but it's beautiful.
Every diver keeps one pearl they'll never sell. Mine's lumpy and grey and the first one I ever grew. Don't you dare laugh.
They say diving's a young merfolk's job. I say it's a stubborn merfolk's job. Different thing entirely.

## diver.heart
The first time I dived to the shelf, I cried the whole way back. Not from fear. It was just so big down there.
When a pearl comes out perfect, I don't want to sell it. I always do. But I sit with it a while first, just the two of us.
I think every pearl remembers the song it was grown to. I hum to mine before I give them away, so they won't be lonely.
I'd dive to the bottom of the deep for a friend. I'd complain the whole way down. But I'd do it.
The deep's taken a few divers over the years. We hang a pearl at the shrine for each of them. That's why the shrine glitters so.
