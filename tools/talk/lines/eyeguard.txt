# guardian-spike gate). Oath: "We watch so the court may close its eye." Terse, steady, protective, very dry.
# They keep the Unseeing (drowned) past the lamps and watch the way to the old halls without ever going.

## eyeguard.chat
We watch so the court may close its eye. That is the oath of the Eyeguard, and the whole of it.
The Unseeing walk the sea floor at night. Your drowned. Two eyes apiece, and not one of them sees anything. We keep them past the lamps.
A trident is not thrown in anger. It is thrown in conclusion.
Sharpening my spines? Not really; they do not need it. But a guard should be seen doing something to them now and then. It reassures the court.
The watch-eye on the post looks out over the deep. It is only a carving. The Unseeing do not know that.
The spike gate is shaped like a guardian's back. In the Long Tide the beasts guarded our halls. Now we guard ourselves, and we borrowed their look.
When a guardian swims too near the court, we do nothing. We watch it. It turns back. They always turn back. I would like to know why. I would also like never to know.
We drill against the dummies every tide. The dummies have never won. They have never lost, either. They are very good at waiting, like the deep.
The Reef Wardens of the merfolk drill with us sometimes. They are fast and loud and hit hard. We are slow and quiet and hit once.
A raised spine is a warning. Two raised spines is a promise. All of them raised is too late.
The best watch is the one where nothing happens. I have had a great many of those. I am very good at my job.
We do not guard against the old halls. Nothing comes out of them. We only watch the direction they lie in. Out of respect. Out of habit. Out of something.
An Eyeguard learns to sleep with the eye half-open. You get used to it. The pillow does not.
Your kind fight with two eyes and still miss. We fight with one and do not. I think you are distracted by having options.
A trident remembers who threw it. That is why it comes home. Mine has come home every time. It is the only loyalty I trust without checking.
The Unseeing were Two-Eyes once, they say, who stayed in the water too long. Keep coming back up. I would hate to have to keep you past the lamps.
The first Eyeguards stood at the Deep Gate during the leaving, to see everyone out. I know every one of their names. Ask me and I will say them all; it takes a while.
Every Eyeguard is given the night watch on the tide of their count. I have stood mine through storms above and silence below. The silence is harder.

## eyeguard.work
Standing guard. You may stand with me, if you are quiet. Most are not quiet. Most leave.
Drilling. Thrust, turn, recover. The water slows every motion, so every motion must mean something.
Watching the ridge. The Unseeing came that way two nights ago. They did not come close. They never come close while the spire burns.
Mending a trident's haft. The prongs are fine. The haft took the blow. That is what hafts are for.
The watch passes from guard to guard at each turn of the tide. Never a gap. Not one tide unwatched since {place} was lit.
If you are going out past the lamps, tell me which way. I will watch that way until you come back.
Counting the lamps on the edge of the court. All lit. Counting them again. All lit. That is the work. It sounds dull. It is the opposite of dull.
The dummy has a crack in its side from my strike last tide. I will apologise to it later.
There is a guardian at the edge of sight, past the ridge. It has been there two tides. It is not coming closer. I am not going further. We understand each other.
Keep clear of the sparring floor while we drill. A trident does not care whether you were invited.
Each Eyeguard keeps a pebble for every watch that passed without trouble. My pile is very tall. I am proud and bored in equal measure.
If you see the Unseeing on your way, tell me where. Do not fight them alone in the dark. They do not tire. You do.
My trident is worn smooth at the grip from my own hand. I would know it blind. In the deep, sometimes, I am.
Climbing the watchtower to the lookout ledge. From up there you can see the dark in every direction. It is a very thorough dark.

## eyeguard.heart
I am not afraid of the Unseeing. I am afraid of the moment I look away. That is why I do not look away.
When the court sleeps, I am the only one awake. It is lonely. It is also an honour. Mostly an honour.
My spines rise before I know why. Afterwards I find the reason. I have never yet found that they were wrong.
I have never had to throw my trident at anything that mattered. I hope that is because of the watch, and not because of luck.
You came back safe. I counted you out and I counted you in. I do not say that to everyone.
Sometimes, on the lookout ledge, I catch myself facing the old halls instead of the court. I turn back round. I do not tell anyone. I have told you.

## eyeguard.held.trident
You hold it too close to the prongs. Back down the haft. There. Now it will throw true.
A good trident. Where did it come from? If from the Unseeing, you have done the sea a service.
Do not throw that in the court. It will come back to you. It will also go through two lamps on the way.
Is it balanced? Let me see you turn it. Hm. Your wrist is honest. The trident will learn the rest.

## eyeguard.held.sword
A blade, in the water? It will be slow. Their hands will be slower. Still: learn the trident.
Keep that sheathed in {place}. Not because I do not trust you. Because I trust it less.
