# THE ELDER (the eldest Tide Singer; conch_shrine; keeps the songs; tells the fry about the Flood).
# Falls back to singer, then merfolk. {elder} is never used here (it names another elder, and there is none).
# Canon: see merfolk.txt. The Elder saw Lowmere's Heart go dark, knows the forty-first verse, and has known
# Old Pell since they were both young.

## elder.greet
Fair currents, child. Oh, don't make that face; at my age, everyone's a child.
Fair currents, {player}. Come and sit by me. My old tail likes company, and my old mouth likes talking.
Fair currents! The fry said a sandtoe had come. I had to see for myself. Yes, those are feet. Marvellous.
Fair currents, and welcome to {place}. I've kept its songs longer than its coral's been growing. Mind you don't trip over any.
Fair currents. You swim the way your grandparents did, I'd wager. All earnest. I like earnest.
Ah, fair currents, {player}. You've come back. Good. I'd started a story for you, and I hate to waste a story.
Fair currents, little sprat. Don't worry, I call everyone that. Even the wardens.
Fair currents. My eyes aren't what they were, but they still know a friendly face. Yours is one.

## elder.chat
I've kept the songs of {place} longer than anyone's kept anything. My tail was gold, once. Now it's more of a distinguished brass.
The Song of the Flood has forty verses and one more that nobody sings. I know it. No, I won't sing it. Not for pearls, not for pickles, not for you.
I tell the fry about the Flood every afternoon. They wriggle through the sad parts and go very still for the scary parts. Just as I did.
Old Pell came through again, telling everyone a squid stole the Barnacle King's crown. Pell's been telling lies longer than I've been telling truths. We're both very good.
When I was a fry, the old Elder told me the Tide sang to the first merfolk as they sank. I've listened for that song my whole life. Some nights I nearly hear it.
You know what the young don't understand? Gossip is how a town remembers. Without it, we'd all be strangers in a year.
Wenna Saltcombe went into the dark with one lantern. Every singer since has carried a little of her fear and a little of her nerve. I've carried mine a long time.
The Pact of Lanterns is older than this reef. The Prismari remember every word of it. We remember the tune. Between us, we've got the whole thing.
I've sung through more Long Cuts and Lantern Nights than I can count. They've all blurred into one lovely, noisy evening.
My scales creak when the current's cold. My voice creaks when I'm tired. My stories never creak. They've had too much practice.
The fry ask if I remember the Flood. I tell them nobody does, but the Song does, and by now I am mostly the Song.
Bell Night is the one night I let myself be sad all the way through. The rest of the year I'm far too busy being wise.
In my day we didn't have Tide Lamps. We had pickles, and we liked them. We told stories in the dark, and the dark made the stories better.
Every Elder before me left one song to the town. I'm still deciding what mine will be. I'm taking my time. I've earned it.
You land folk have such short memories and such long legs. We've the opposite. It all evens out.

## elder.work
My work is keeping the songs. All of them. The happy ones, the sad ones, and "The Nautilus Who Wouldn't". Especially that one; the fry demand it.
Today I'm teaching the fry the third verse of the Song of the Flood. They'll forget it by supper. I'll teach it again tomorrow. That's how songs survive.
I keep the book of the Song here in my hall, in Prismari ink on shell-paper. Wet through and still clear. Read it if you like. Don't drip on it. Well. Don't drip more.
An Elder keeps the songs, settles quarrels, names the fry, and gets the best spot at the shrine. It's mostly the last one.
I sing to the Heart at dawn, before the other singers are up. It knows my voice best of all. We're old friends, the Heart and I.
When two merfolk quarrel, they come to me. I listen, I hum, and I tell them a story about two fish who quarrelled. By the end they've forgotten what they were angry about.
I name every fry in {place}. The family suggests; I decide. I've only been wrong once, and that fry grew into the name anyway.
I lead the Bell Night vigil. Every name, every peal. I know them all by heart. It's the heaviest thing I carry, and I carry it gladly.
On Lantern Night I greet the Prismari envoy. "I see you," they say. "And I am seen," I say. Then we sing until the coral shakes.
I keep the town's memory. Who courted whom, who fell out, who stole whose pickles in which year. Don't make me tell you. I will.
The young singers come to me when a song won't sit right. I sing it back to them slowly, and they hear where it went crooked.
Some days my work is just floating at the shrine, being old where everyone can see. It reminds them we've been here a long while, and that we'll go on.
I still brew a little for the old ones' aches. Kelp, pickle, a long hum. Tastes awful. Works beautifully.
I keep a list of every visitor from the dry. You're on it now. I gave you a little star. Don't let it go to your head.
I tell the fry the Flood story so they'll never be afraid of the water, and so they'll never forget the land. Both. That's the trick.

## elder.heart
I've sung the Bell Night names so many years, I sometimes think the lost ones know my voice. I hope it comforts them. It comforts me.
When I go, the songs will go on in someone else. That's all right. That's the whole point of a song.
I've watched every fry in {place} blush their colour. Every single one. There's no greater joy, in the sea or out of it.
I'm not afraid of the deep, child. I've lived long enough to know it's just more sea, and the sea has always been kind to me.
You remind me of a story I haven't told in years. A kind one. Come back some evening and I'll tell it to you properly.

## elder.past
When I was young, the old Elder sang the forty-first verse once, alone, out at the shelf. They didn't speak for a week after, and never sang it again. I've never asked what that verse does.
My gran told me her gran remembered the blankets. When the first merfolk came ashore looking for their families, the land folk brought blankets. Remember the blankets, child.
Lowmere was still lit when I was a fry. I remember its Heart on the edge of the dark, a little blue star. Then one night it wasn't there.
In my youth we raced nautili without saddles. Just a fist on the shell and a prayer. The shellwrights call that foolish now. It was. It was glorious.
I saw Old Coil once, when I was smaller than my own fluke. She looked at me with one great eye and went on her way. I've been lucky ever since.
I met Old Pell when we were both young, and Pell was already a liar. Told me the moon was a pearl the Tide was growing. I believed it for years. I half believe it still.
Every Elder before me is in a song. When I go, they'll sing me too. I've asked for something cheerful. With a chorus.
I remember the pickle moon, the year of the great storm, when the kelp tore loose and we ate nothing but sea pickles. I couldn't look at a pickle for a year. Now I'm fond of them again.
I was there the last time an Oldest Eye came up from the deep for Lantern Night. They looked at our light a long while and said "it is enough". We wept like fry.
My first Bell Night as a singer, my voice broke on the twelfth name. The old Elder sang it for me, then handed the song back. That's how it's done. You catch each other.

## elder.joke
Why did the old merfolk cross the reef? To tell the young ones they were doing it wrong. Ha! That's me. I'm the old merfolk.
I asked Old Pell how old Pell was. "Older than the Song," says Pell. "Then sing me the forty-first verse," says I. Pell went very quiet. Best joke I ever told.
What's the difference between an Elder and a storyteller? About forty verses and a good deal of shame.
The fry asked if I was around before the Flood. I said yes, I'm the one who left the well-bucket out. They still don't know if I'm joking.
A young singer asked me the secret of a long life. "Never whistle over the drop, never eat a lit pickle, and never argue with a nautilus." They wrote it down. Good student.
