## warden.chat
The drowned are the ones the Tide wouldn't take. Slow, heavy, and they don't stop. We pity them. We still poke them with tridents.
"Hold the arch." That's the Warden's Oath. Stand in the archway and nothing gets past you into the town. Simple to say. Hard on a long night.
The Barnacle King? Fry story. A great drowned with a crown of barnacles, sitting on the shrine of lost Lowmere. Fry story. I keep my trident sharp anyway.
A trident's better than a sword down here. It goes through water like it was made for it. Swords drag. Swords sulk.
I ride the reef on my nautilus every afternoon: round the edges, past the gardens, along the shelf. It pretends it's a chore. It isn't; it loves it.
The first wardens fought the drowned with fence posts from the old villages. We've got tridents now. I think about those fence posts and feel very lucky.
The drowned come at night, mostly. They follow the lights. That's why the lights are inside the arches and we're outside them.
If a drowned has a trident, take it. That's the rule. Anything they'd use against us, we take back and use better.
I've polished this trident so often I can see my tail in it. Gorgeous tail. Good trident.
The guardians at the old monuments don't bother us so long as we don't bother them. That's the best kind of neighbour.
Night watch is quiet. Mostly. You get to know every shadow on the reef. Then a new shadow turns up and your scales go stiff.
We train the fry to swim fast and loud. A loud fry is a safe fry; we can always find them.
I saw Old Coil once, on night watch. White, huge, slow, out past the shelf. I didn't tell anyone for a moon. Who believes a tired warden?
Every scratch on my armour's a story. That one's a drowned. That one's a guardian. That one's a very angry clam.
The deep doesn't scare me. The shallows at night, when the lamps flicker... those scare me a bit.

## warden.work
Drilling with the trident: thrust, twist, pull back, check the arch. A thousand times. Then a thousand more.
I keep watch on the arches until midnight, every other night. The other nights, I lie awake listening anyway.
My job's simple: no drowned past the arches. Everything else is gossip and polishing.
I train on the dummy every morning. It's never once complained, and it's won a few. Don't tell anyone.
Riding the reef is half the work. From a nautilus you spot a drowned early. From the ground you spot it late.
If the conch blows twice, I'm the first thing you'll see. Then the drowned, behind me, regretting things.
I check every lamp at dusk. A dark lamp is a gap, and a gap is where the drowned come through.
The singers brew me night-eyes for the watch. The dark goes grey and the drowned go bright. Tastes like old kelp, but it works.
I teach the fry to throw tridents. Little ones, blunt. They're terrifying. One of them got me in the tail last week. So proud.
A warden keeps their trident sharp, their armour tight and their nautilus fed. In that order, unless the nautilus is hungry. Then the nautilus.
When the drowned come in numbers, the conch sounds and every warden swims for the arches. We've never lost one yet. The arch, I mean. Well, either.
I patrol the gardens too. Seadragons can't fight. They just pretend to be kelp and hope. Somebody has to hope for them.
Training's not about being strong. It's about being where you're needed before you're needed there.
My armour is shell over leather over hope. The shellwright says the hope's the weakest layer. I say it's the most important.
Want to help? Keep your eyes open on the shelf road, and tell me if anything grey is walking where it shouldn't.

## warden.heart
I don't love fighting. I love {place}. Sometimes loving it means fighting. That's the trick of it.
Every drowned I turn away was somebody once. I say "rest easy" when I send them off. They can't hear me. I say it anyway.
On long watches I think about everyone asleep behind the arches. Then the dark doesn't feel so big.
I was scared on my first night watch. I'm still scared, a bit, every time. Brave isn't not being scared. Brave is staying at the arch.
If anything ever came for you here, {player}, it would have to come through me first. I want you to know that.
