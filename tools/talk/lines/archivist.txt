I see you. You are the first visitor of this tide. I have written it down, so you are now history.

## archivist.chat
We record every tide: its height, its pull, its temper. A tide nobody writes down is a tide that never happened, and we do not allow that.
The Reckoning of Tides is the first book every young one reads. One tide, two tides, twenty-eight, thirteen turnings. After that it is all arithmetic and feelings.
Our books are written very small. A Long Count fits on a page. The Long Tide fills the Archive. The Silence fills one shelf, and nobody reads it.
The barred shelf is sealed by the Oldest Eye. The sign says do not read. I have kept it for many turnings and I have never read it. I have also never stopped thinking about it.
The bars on the sealed shelf are not there to keep the books in.
We keep the sea's own books: ones that teach the lungs patience, the hands to work in the deep, the feet to swim as we do, and a trident to come home. The Eyeguards borrow that last kind constantly.
A book that calls the sky's fire down on a trident's point. A book that coaxes fish to the hook. We keep them all, written in the old way. The words are older than the halls.
An Archivist's highest praise is "accurate". The second highest is "legible". I have given the second, twice.
I have been reading The Halls We Left again. Every time, I want to add a footnote. Every time, I am not allowed.
Two-Eyes write in straight lines across the page. We write in rings, from the edge inward, like the eye. You finish a book. We arrive at it.
Do not ask an Archivist a question unless you have a tide to spare. We have no short answers. We have short chapters.
Every hall's name is kept here. Some we know only by their first letter. We keep that letter as carefully as a whole name.
Glow ink for the tides, plain ink for the margins. That way the important things can be read in the dark, and the opinions cannot.
The merfolk keep their history in songs. We keep ours in books. Their songs change a little every time they are sung. Our books never change. I am no longer sure which is wiser.
When a young one first feels the pull, an Archivist writes down their count. It is the first line about them in the record. The young ones are all first lines.
I once spent three turnings checking a single tide. It was correct. I was very pleased, and very tired, and it was correct.
Every Long Count, every book here is copied once. Nothing in this Archive is older than its last copy, and nothing in it is younger than its first.
Ossiveth, the last Oldest Eye of the halls, sealed the first record of the Silence. Every Oldest since has sealed it again. The seals are very neat. The shelf has a lot of them.
"Count the tide, not the wave." A good proverb. The Archive's addition: "But record the wave, in case." We are not popular at gatherings.

## archivist.work
Recording the tides. The pull came early today. A hair's breadth, but early. I have written it down, with a small, disapproving mark.
Copying a page that has grown faint. You copy slowly, so the old hand and the new hand agree on every letter.
I am cataloguing books the merfolk brought down from the shallows. Most are about the land. One is about cooking. I have filed it under fiction.
If you have a book of the sea, show me. If you want one, I may part with one, for a fair trade and a promise to read it.
Reading by lamplight. Not for pleasure. Well. Not only for pleasure.
The count for {place}: four hundred and twelve Long Counts since the leaving, and some turnings over. I could tell you to the tide. Shall I? No? Most people say no.
Do not touch the chiseled shelves. Each slot is a book's place, and each book knows its place better than you or I.
Today's record: the tide, the pull, the light of the spire, the visitors. You are under "visitors". You are the whole of "visitors".
An Archivist reads three books at once: one to learn from, one to check it against, and one to stay awake for the other two.
I am cross-checking two old ledgers. One says a tide was calm, the other says stormy, and both are in the same hand. I suspect a bad day.
If you find any writing in the old halls, any at all, bring it here. Do not read it first. Bring it.
I am mending a margin note a predecessor left. It says "nonsense" in very small letters. I agree. I am writing it larger.
Every evening I read the day's record aloud to the empty Archive. Nobody listens. That is not the point. The point is that it was said.
I am writing out a new copy of The Reckoning of Tides for the young. It is the third this Long Count. They keep reading it to pieces. I could not be prouder.

## archivist.heart
I am afraid, sometimes, that I will write something wrong, and nobody will notice, and it will become true by being old.
I will never read the sealed shelf. But some nights I stand in front of the bars and wonder whether whoever wrote it wanted it read.
The names we have lost trouble me more than the halls. A hall is only stone. A name was someone.
I would like, once, to be written down myself. Not as a count; as a sentence. "They kept the record well." That would be enough.
You ask questions as if the answers matter. In the Archive, that is the only qualification there is.
When I stop counting, another Archivist will write one line about me and then go on recording the tide. That seems right to me. The tide is more important. I hope the line is accurate.

## archivist.held.book
A book! May I? Gently. Ah, land paper; it drinks the sea. Hold it close and swim it home quickly.
Is that one of ours? Some of the sea's books travel a long way. If it is, it has come back to tell us about it.
What does it say? No, do not tell me. Let me guess from how you hold it. Something about the land. Something with a sad ending.
If you ever tire of that book, the Archive will give it a shelf, a number, and a very long life.

## archivist.held.map
A map! Hold it still. I will copy the part with the sea on it. It will not take long; there is very little.
Your map is drawn by the sun and the stars. Ours would be counted in tides. Shall we trade? You will find ours very hard to read, and I yours.
