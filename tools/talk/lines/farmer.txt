## farmer.chat
Kelp grows faster than anything in the sea. Turn your back for a day and it's taller than you. Turn it for a week and it's taller than the shrine.
Drying kelp's the one thing we need the sun for. We carry it up to the sun-rocks at low tide and lay it out. Best part of the job, honestly. Warm rocks!
A good kelp wrap is fish, kelp and patience. Mostly patience. You roll it tight, then you roll it again.
The seadragons hide in my rows pretending to be kelp. I've harvested two by accident. Both forgave me.
My gran's sea-pickle wrap is famous across three reefs. The secret is a pickle in the middle. That's the whole secret. Don't tell anyone.
The Long Cut's the best day of the year. Everyone hauls, everyone sings "Haul the Green", everyone eats until they float sideways.
Sea pickles grow in clusters, four to a cluster when they're happy. One on its own is lonely. I move it next to the others.
Fish wander into my rows and get lost. I lead them out by the fins. They never say thank you.
Kelp needs sand under it and water over it. That's it. That's the whole of farming. The rest is worrying.
I tried growing a land thing down here once. A potato. It just sat there looking betrayed.
The turtles come through my rows some moons and nibble the tips. I let them. Turtles were here long before farms.
The trick is height. Short kelp's sweet, tall kelp's tough. Wraps want the sweet stuff; ropes want the tough.
When the current's strong, all the kelp leans one way like it's listening to something. I listen too. Never hear a thing.
People think kelp farmers are dull. We're not. We're patient. It just looks the same from outside.
Fresh kelp keeps a day, dried kelp keeps a year, and a good friend keeps forever. That's not farming advice. I just thought of it.

## farmer.work
Tending the rows today: thinning the tall ones, coaxing the short ones, scolding the ones that lean.
Hanging kelp on the racks is half the job. You hang it, you turn it, you take it up to the sun-rocks to finish, and then you turn it again.
I sell dried kelp, kelp wraps, sea pickles and fish. Basically, I sell supper.
A kelp rack wants hanging from the top down, longest strands first. My gran would rap my knuckles if I did it backwards.
The rows by the shelf grow tallest but bitterest. The rows by the Heart grow sweetest. Nobody knows why. Everyone has a theory.
Want to help? Grab a bundle and swim it to the racks. Don't stop to gossip on the way. You will, but try.
Fish are a kelp farmer's business too. They live in the rows, I get to know them, and then one day it's a wrap. That's life in the green.
Sea pickles are my side crop. They're lamps, they're snacks, they're presents. Every home in {place} has some of mine.
I sing "Haul the Green" while I work. Everyone in the rows sings it. It keeps us together, and it keeps the fish out of the way.
The worst job is untangling the kelp after a storm up top. The whole field ties itself in knots overnight, out of spite.
Some days the seadragons follow me down the rows like ducklings. I don't know what ducklings are, but a land cousin said so, and I liked the sound.
A good harvest feeds the town. A great harvest feeds the town and the next reef over. We've had three great ones running. Touch coral.
Never cut kelp at the root. Cut it a hand above the sand. It grows back by the next moon, and it remembers you were kind.
Dried kelp is what the town lives on when the fish move off. I dry more than we need every moon. Better a full rack than an empty belly.
I'm teaching the fry to plant. They plant everything upside down. It grows anyway. Kelp's forgiving.

## farmer.heart
I like knowing everybody in {place} ate something I grew today. It's a quiet sort of pride. It fills you up.
These rows were my gran's before they were mine. I still talk to her when I'm working them. Kelp's a good listener.
A bad harvest scares me more than the drowned. You can fight a drowned. You can't fight an empty rack.
Some evenings I just float in the rows and let the kelp sway round me. It's the safest I ever feel.
If you ever go hungry up in the dry, come down. There'll be a wrap for you. There'll always be a wrap for you.
